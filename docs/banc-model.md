# BANC model notes

## Equations and units

Neurons use mV, ms, pF, nS and pA. Each incoming chemical edge reads its source's release from a delay ring sized to the maximum addressed delay plus one (up to 32 slots). A spiking source releases `1/dt` for its threshold step; a graded source releases `0.1 * sigmoid((V - midpoint)/slope)` per ms. Transmission delays are at least one step, so reads and writes never alias the same ring slot within a dispatch.

For each of nine receptor classes, delayed weighted release supplies `D`. Two sequential low-pass states update as `rise += (D-rise)*(1-exp(-dt/tau_rise))`, then `gate += (rise-gate)*(1-exp(-dt/tau_decay))`. The first five classes supply conductance with their configured reversal potentials. The last four supply bounded monoamine occupancies `gate/(1+gate)` that affect only neurons with nonzero configured sensitivity. These cascades approximate kinetics; they are not fitted molecular binding models.

Membrane voltage uses a semi-implicit conductance update. Leak, chemical reversal terms and optional electrical neighbor voltages contribute to the numerator; total conductance contributes to the denominator. Adaptive current decays exponentially and increments on spikes. Spiking neurons reset and observe a refractory period. Graded neurons do neither. External input and tonic current are modulated by monoamine sensitivity; an explicit per-cell coefficient admits hunger, AKH-like and insulin-like state. Voltage is bounded to [-100, 60] mV to make extreme parameter failures bounded, not to assert biophysical fidelity.

The [current interface audit](../reports/banc-interface-audit/README.md) finds that the chemical modulation gain is ineffective in production: all 5,357 gain-bearing motor/Kenyon cells have zero tonic current and no external sensory input. Their gain therefore multiplies zero. Kinetic gates and neuromodulator neurons exist, but this equation/configuration does not make them influence the motor or Kenyon membrane dynamics. The separate olfactory hunger/hormone term, metabolism and muscle fuel remain active. Correcting this requires a defined physiological modulation target; no replacement equation has been silently substituted.

Chemical input uses observed edge contact counts and a provisional global conversion of 0.1 nS·ms per contact. A five-value sweep selected this gain for silent motor outputs under zero external input while retaining evoked proboscis/pump activity under sugar stimulation. The earlier 0.5 gain produced sustained wing activity with every external sense disabled. This two-constraint calibration is recorded in `reports/banc-rest-calibration.json`; it is not a physiological fit or validation of sensory selectivity. A verified transmitter annotation takes precedence over its prediction. Predicted glutamate defaults to inhibitory GluCl in the CNS. This is a prior, not an inference of receptor expression. An override `{pre, post, receptor, source}` can select another receptor for an existing edge. Unknown transmitters keep their edge and count but have zero conductance until assigned explicitly.

Electrical pairs are a separate table. Two type-identified left/right GF–PSI pairs follow [published giant-fiber physiology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4833477/). Their 0.02 nS coupling and symmetric approximation are assumed. Actual giant-fiber synapses can rectify; rectification and a calibrated DLM electrical network remain to be implemented. No gap junction is inferred simply from a chemical edge. The broader [flight motor gap-junction result](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06099-0) motivates future calibration, but does not justify assigning a complete electrical network from EM alone.

## Muscle and body boundary

Only `super_class == motor` annotations with explicit target, function and laterality enter the muscle map. Descending cells are never accepted by this adapter. Antagonistic leg groups supply normalized force differences to native position actuators. Long-tendon targets engage native claw adhesion; tibia–tarsus targets control tarsus joints. Wing power groups use DLM/DVM targets. Wing steering and proboscis grouping still require calibration of signs and moment arms. Unmapped targets remain neural readouts with no actuator effect.

Muscle activation follows distinct rise and decay constants. A slowly changing fatigue variable, normalized energy availability, a bell-shaped force–length relation and bounded force–velocity relation scale contractile force. An unfitted rate scale of 80 Hz maps to full activation. The force difference shifts the native leg joint target by at most 0.35 radians around the starting stance, bounded by the native joint limits; MuJoCo applies the resulting actuator forces. This excursion is a modeling prior, not a fitted motor-unit moment arm. Muscle forces can outlast the last spike or a coupling disconnect through their decay state. Wing-force disconnection is a separate mechanical intervention.

Energy is a reserve rather than a linear multiplier on strength: fuel availability is `clamp(energy / 0.1, 0, 1)`. Above the assumed depletion threshold, hunger does not itself prevent contraction. Its threshold remains an unfitted modeling prior.

The default body runs the actual [MuJoCo WASM solver](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/blob/main/wasm/README.md), version 3.13.0. The mesh-free export of pinned [FlyBody](https://github.com/TuragaLab/flybody) preserves compiled per-body inertia and total mass, native collision primitives, friction, actuator dynamics and ellipsoid wing aerodynamics. It retains 50 joint coordinates and freezes 52, with all six root coordinates active. There are 56 native actuators including six claws. It uses FlyBody's cm, g and seconds, with display lengths scaled to millimeters. The bowl uses a heightfield; 189 separate static fruit solids follow the original rendered banana and apple meshes, preserving empty space beneath fruit. Microscopic claw geometry remains approximate. [Native/render contact checks](../reports/flybody-habitat-collision-validation.md) quantify this habitat representation.

Leg motor groups modulate native position actuators. These servos preserve a passive target posture at zero neural drive; gravity and settling can still move a disconnected body. Wing power activation selects calibrated waveforms at 235.813447 Hz. The wing joints use FlyBody's position-error-to-force interface and native fluid-force model. A whole-body center-of-mass torque correction and numerical steering-response fits improve isolated mechanics, but do not establish stable flight in the actual BANC loop. The [wing correction report](../reports/flybody-wing-correction/README.md) separates measured source quantities from these fits and model assumptions. These actuator mappings and the wing pattern are the explicit model gap; the direct BANC path does not use the separately available learned reference policy. No desired root velocity, height, heading, food bearing or task phase enters this adapter.

Initialization adjusts the native leg posture to the local terrain using bounded inverse kinematics. It only sets the initial pose and resting actuator targets; it supplies no gait, stabilization or in-flight root commands. The six root coordinates remain free. Quiet flat-floor support is tested separately from unstable active behavior on curved fruit.

The original two-segment visual leg meshes follow physical limb landmarks and native claw sites. The visual thorax center is registered to the physical root with the full quaternion, including the root-to-mesh offset. A world-vertical offset previously separated the body and feet when tilted. Frozen native antennae now supply their fixed posture and zero joint-motion feedback rather than the earlier display oscillator. The earlier quadratic-foot/diagonal-inertia surrogate remains available in the separate research lab; it is no longer the default console body. The original assisted behavior mode remains a comparison and still has finite food and internal-state updates.

## Ground and joint sensing

The native contact solver supplies normal load for each of the six legs, normalized by body weight. Only actual environment contacts enter that load. Joint motion does not create a contact signal. BANC organ, side and modality annotations assign 61 campaniform load inputs, 2,976 leg-bristle touch inputs, 403 position inputs, 149 velocity inputs, 403 vibration inputs and 449 haltere/wing-base rotation inputs. These remain separate neuron inputs even though the original UI summarizes eight channels. A worker barrier requires body feedback for each 2 ms neural block, independently of rendering. The assisted kinematic comparison retains its original sensory adapter.

`web/banc-ground-sense.js` declares the numerical tuning priors: load scales at 80 Hz per body weight, leg-shaft contact at 60 Hz, joint velocity at 3 Hz per rad/s, and position at a 5 Hz baseline plus absolute tibia/coxa angles. Rotation input uses angular speed and wing activation and remains active when the feet are unloaded. Rates are capped at 100 Hz. Direction-selective tuning, adaptation, individual receptor thresholds and haltere mechanics are not fitted. Leg-shaft collisions stand in for individual bristle contacts. This provides ground information to the sensory neurons; it does not establish that the current neural parameters produce a corrective posture response.

## Internal state and task observation

Food is a finite normalized reservoir. In direct mode, ingestion requires actual native mouth contact with a fruit solid, proboscis activation and pumping activation. Foot contact, hovering above food, or bowl contact under projected fruit is insufficient. Taste routing uses the contacted native geometry and annotated organ/side; FlyBody's simplified mouth geometry remains an approximation. The assisted comparison retains its original feeding boundary. Accepted intake enters the crop, then is absorbed into energy. Basal and muscular expenditure consume energy. Crop/energy determine hunger; insulin-like and AKH-like variables follow slower counter-regulatory targets. [The glucose-sensing hormonal circuit](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1675-4) motivates that qualitative relationship. No metabolic rate or circulating hormone concentration here is a measured physiological quantity.

The task monitor reads behavior. It never writes current or motor drive. Localization is an orientation proxy requiring an initially misaligned body to turn toward food in the presence of odor; mere detection does not pass. Approach requires at least a body length (0.23 cm) of distance reduction over 200 ms while outside food contact. Flight requires 250 ms of contact-free, powered, upright motion with bounded mean descent/rotation and kinematic support against gravity. Landing requires a previously qualified flight followed by 100 ms of stable upright food contact with bounded approach speed and contact motion. Probing requires extended proboscis contact, feeding requires actual intake, and takeoff requires upward departure above 0.5 cm/s with active wings after feeding. The thresholds are modeled observation criteria, not measured biological limits. These independent event checks do not establish the whole ordered sequence or causal neural coding.

## What remains scientifically unresolved

The implemented model can be run and perturbed, but successful autonomous behavior is not established. A useful next calibration dataset would pair identified cell electrophysiology, receptor expression, stimulus-response recordings and identified motor-unit output with reduced-body joint/force trajectories. Fit those parameters against held-out experiments, then evaluate repeated complete trials with shuffled-connectivity, sensory-off, VNC-lesion and motor-disconnection controls. A successful motion trace alone is insufficient.

Current limits include unresolved electrical topology/rectification, broad default physiology, transmitter uncertainty, uncalibrated sensory tuning, unknown neuromodulator receptor expression, reduced muscle geometry, unfitted actuator calibration, approximate habitat geometry and simplified wing aerodynamics. The original RGB cameras, FlyVis front end and anatomical viewer are retained in the 3D console with BANC-specific indices and anatomy. Representative-point retinotopy is an approximation. Reference FlyWire data remain available separately for comparison.

## Compute boundary

WebGPU owns persistent neural state, kinetics and delay history. Each dispatch reads the previous state and writes the next. Each step has a distinct aligned uniform range; a queue write to a reused uniform cannot accidentally give every dispatch the final step index. Incoming CSR avoids floating-point atomic synaptic accumulation. Readout dispatches gather only requested neurons before staging readback. WGSL/C++ parity is checked on mixed chemical/electrical, spiking/graded circuits and across workgroup boundaries.

For selected-neuron voltage recording, the command batch copies the eight state fields after each dispatch into a small staging buffer. Mapping once at the end preserves every reference/fast timestep without submitting a separate population reduction and CPU round trip for each sample. Tests cover odd/even state-buffer swaps and compare complete per-step GPU traces against WASM.

WASM runs the fallback neural equations, muscle activation and native MuJoCo body dynamics. JavaScript coordinates the GPU API and environment. The runtime reports initialization fallback and stops on device loss. It never claims GPU execution when running WASM. Performance reports name the adapter and distinguish kernel compute from graph loading and readback.
