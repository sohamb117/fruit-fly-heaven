# Existing feedback and the limits of the 27-parameter search

This is a read-only code/report review; no neural or body simulation was run. The inspected legacy bundle enables event-driven wing muscles, the ten-cell ionic DLM profile, and tegula load feedback; `haltereFeedback` is absent and vision is off. “Legacy” here does **not** mean that wings still use the former 50 ms rate decoder.

The newly completed maintained-flight baseline must be interpreted first: after its 500 ms restrained setup, its first sampled environment contact is at scored time 374 ms, COM height 5.609 cm against a 5.6 cm ceiling. Its first tilt beyond 45° is at 380 ms, after contact. It earns 324 ms of qualified flight and terminates at 524 ms. The pre-contact record shows sustained upward velocity and full wing power. This run does not establish that missing directional feedback caused its first flight loss. Height/lift regulation, arena clearance, and attitude stabilization are distinct questions. ## What reaches the baseline brain

| Native quantity | Actual encoding | Information retained or lost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Root angular velocity, rad/s | Each rotation afferent gets `min(100, 5*activity + 4*sidePower*norm(omega))` Hz | Organ and side remain; explicit axis and sign are discarded. Same organ/side cells get the same rate. At power/activity 1, saturation begins at 23.75 rad/s. |
| Body orientation | Antenna fallback includes `10*acos(up)` Hz | Unsigned tilt only. Signed bank/pitch and gravity direction are available in the body object but are not directly projected into sensory neurons. |
| Translation | Antenna fallback includes `2*min(speed,30)` Hz; speed is display mm/s | Speed magnitude only, with the term capped at 60 Hz. No direct height, vertical-speed sign, or ceiling-distance input. |
| Antennae | Side-specific absolute angle and nonnegative/clipped speed terms | Separate sides, but no explicit root-axis tuning. The current prepared manifest leaves 249 left and 330 right antenna cells on this fallback. |
| Wings / tegula | Each of 26 cells gets side-specific `100*load/(0.1390686725503348+load)` Hz | Side-specific native fluid-load magnitude; all cells within a side share a value. This is a declared load proxy, not measured receptor strain or directional tuning. |
| Legs / contact | Native mapped load, touch, vibration, and absolute joint-angle/velocity terms | Side/leg identities and contact/load differences remain. Sign is removed from the mapped joint velocities and angles. The 50 unsupported proximal hairplate fallback cells now get zero added host current. |
| Food | Bilateral odor samples and organ-specific contact taste | Position/heading/contact can supply indirect state information. Vision is explicitly disabled. |

The legacy haltere activity term may include haltere steering-muscle force even when asynchronous hDVM power is zero; its rotation-dependent term still vanishes at zero hDVM power. Wing-base afferents use their own side's wing power. Missing/zero side power never borrows the opposite side.

This is **not** a proof that every positive/negative perturbation generates identical future inputs. If only the sign of the current angular-rate field is changed while the rest of a saved sensory sample stays fixed, the direct rotation mapper cannot distinguish it. Actual perturbed physics can subsequently change bilateral tegula loads, contacts, odor location, joint state and recurrent history. Those indirect routes could carry useful signs. No current report measures a stable corrective transfer through those routes.

## Units and the sensor-to-motor path

Ordinary sensory values are **requested Hz**, converted to **added pA** by profile-specific, isolated-neuron calibration in the production WASM equations. The calibration has no incoming chemical/gap inputs and zero internal modulation. Requested Hz therefore does not guarantee that rate in the recurrent full graph. Positive requests below 2 Hz use the mapper's 2 Hz floor; zero requests add zero current. Intrinsic current, receptor modulation, internal state and adaptation still act inside the neuron. State slot 7 includes those terms and is not a measurement of raw external sensory current.

Prepared chemical weights are integrated nS·ms, with delayed release passed through receptor rise/decay kinetics. Membrane conductances are nS, voltage mV, capacitance pF and current pA. Existing audited direct tegula→B1 and selected haltere→B1 edges are nAChR, 2 ms delay, 0.3/3 ms rise/decay. The exact nine-cell CL3 annotation subset contains seven same-side B1 chemical edges, totaling 1.8 nS·ms left and 2.7 right. No configured electrical edge touches either B1 or that subset. These are existing graph facts, not a justification to invent an electrical connection. [B1 route audit](../flight-b1-mapping/README.md), [tegula budget](../flight-tegula-inputs/b1-budget/README.md).

The environment samples physical feedback every 2 ms, advances BANC through four 0.5 ms ticks, reads actual wing events, and advances the body. Ten DLM cells use the explicit 0.1 ms ionic substep. For the 48 wing MNs, exact events feed 28 muscle mappings through causal 1 ms excitation integration and the native activation/fatigue/force kernel. The 50 ms filtered MN-rate readout remains diagnostic for these wing groups. It still drives non-wing muscles, including both hDVM haltere power muscles, via the rate/80 clamp and native activation dynamics. Thus the event repair did not remove the hDVM recruitment delay or its side-specific zero drive.

## What the mechanical-haltere option adds

The opt-in mapper replaces exactly 328 haltere external-current entries, leaving wing-base afferents on their legacy mapping. It uses root-local signed angular velocity, wing phase/frequency and each side's hDVM force-derived power. A virtual oscillating point mass supplies inertial bending and the signed Coriolis term `-2*m*(omega cross velocity)`. Per-cell bending projections are rectified and mapped directly to pA by `800*c/(1+c)`; they bypass the ordinary rate-to-current calibration. Geometry, sensitivity, amplitude/phase and the exchangeable receptive orientations are explicit modeling priors, not measured cell tuning.

Angular velocity and power are held across each 2 ms body block; phase advances for its four 0.5 ms neural inputs. The option therefore adds phase-dependent signed information, but neither a gravity/attitude vector nor direct altitude regulation. Zero hDVM power means zero virtual motion and zero current. It also replaces any legacy baseline haltere activity current, so this intervention is not simply “legacy plus a signed term.”

The [pure mechanical observability report](../flight-haltere-prototype/observability/README.md) finds full-cycle bilateral rank three but strong conditioning dependence; unilateral examples are very poorly conditioned. These are virtual load/current matrices, not measured brain or body controllability. The large phase-dependent baseline bending also exceeds the tested Coriolis component; neuron saturation and sampling can alter usable sensitivity.

## What the saved response evidence establishes

- **Current live pathway:** the [nonperturbing neural observer](../flight-haltere-neural-observation/README.md) records zero left hDVM spikes/power in both baseline and mechanical runs. The mechanical run has zero left haltere sensory spikes, while all 157 right cells become active. This limits that observed implementation; it does not establish biological absence of left motion or an incorrect neuron identity.
- **Current positive sensory control:** prescribing left virtual motion after 100 ms recruits left B1; its first post-onset event occurs at 111 ms for 18° and 108 ms for 36°, versus 146 ms in the zero-motion arm. In the common (100,720] ms window, left B1 counts increase 16→71→98. This demonstrates a responsive path in the present ionic/event system, not isolated synaptic latency or correctly signed corrective torque. Both interventions reduce best flight from 504 to 440 ms. [Matched analysis](../flight-haltere-motion-control/README.md).
- **Historical identification:** frozen-context organ/side pulses produced early cumulative MN-count changes at 8–24 ms, but polarity and gain were state-dependent across 100/300 ms conditioning. Sparse transient counts survived some comparisons; zero endpoint contrast did not rule out timing codes. These tests used older generic-DLM/rate/Hill output conditions and no integrated body, so their muscle-force ranks are not a current stabilizing controller. [Robustness analysis](../flight-afferent-identification/robustness/AGGREGATE.md).
- **Plant capability:** the separate explicit-feedback synthetic event controller sustained its prescribed airborne test for 1.5 s, unlike fixed open-loop commands. That proves useful mechanical authority under its own initialization/controller, not that BANC currently provides those commands. [Positive control](../flight-event-control/README.md).

## What 27 positive, bilateral-tied gains can express

The three global coordinates change power, deployment time and frequency. The other 24 scale each muscle type's fixed angle-bias and amplitude basis, identically for left/right homologues while retaining their separate measured forces. They can adjust static trim and the strength of an existing temporal/bilateral response. Because the fixed mechanical basis itself has signed components and opposing muscles, positive gains do **not** imply only positive body torque.

They cannot add a missing sensory variable, select a receptor axis, learn a new neural connection, independently tune homologues, reverse one basis vector's sign, prescribe a new spike phase, or create a nonzero contribution from a muscle whose current force is exactly zero. Changing mechanics may alter later feedback/recruitment, so zero force at a fixed sample does not prove a parameter useless for all trajectories. The [trainability audit](../flight-development-v8-staging/trainability-review/README.md) specifically found both B1 homologues zero in only 20/95 qualified sparse frames, not universal B1 silence; its rank-12 shape Jacobian is not a body-torque or stabilization proof.

The defensible conclusion is conditional: these gains could fit stabilization if the existing neural signals already contain suitable corrective signs/timing, or if the plant is sufficiently passively stable. Current evidence has not shown that condition, and scaling cannot repair missing information by itself. For the newly released airborne baseline, ceiling contact currently prevents diagnosing later free-flight attitude behavior; claiming a neural feedback failure from that crash would overstate the evidence. The next interpretation should keep contact-free lift/height behavior, motor authority, and neural corrective response separate. No new experiment or parameter change is performed or selected by this review.
