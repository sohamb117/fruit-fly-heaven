# Flight motor timing: primary evidence and interface boundary

Bounded review, 2026-09-14. No runtime or canonical model changes.

## Direct Drosophila measurements

| Study and preparation | Measurement | Meaning and limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Trimarchi & Murphey 1997](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6573327/), adult Drosophila, electrical stimulation of haltere afferents | Strong stimulation evoked b1 muscle EMGs at **1.72 ± 0.16 ms**; all such wild-type responses were below **2.1 ms**. Weaker stimulation and shaking-B mutants had longer, variable latencies. | A fast sensory-to-muscle electrical pathway exists. This is stimulation-to-EMG latency, not twitch rise/decay, and does not specify a universal delay for all afferents. |
| [Heide & Götz 1996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8708578/), tethered Drosophila, visual optomotor stimulation with muscle recordings | b1 fires approximately **once per wingbeat**, within a narrow phase band near the upstroke/downstroke transition. Advancing b1 phase accompanies increased wingbeat amplitude. b2 and i1 show burst activity at distinct stroke phases. | Rate alone cannot describe b1's observed phase modulation. This is physiological correlation, not a full mechanical transfer function for every steering muscle. |
| [Lehmann & Götz 1996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8785006/), tethered Drosophila, b2 stimulation at selected wingbeat phases | b2 bursts near **100 Hz**, about one spike per two strokes. Changing stimulus phase changes wingbeat-amplitude efficacy. The most effective stimulus precedes the normal muscle-spike band by about **1 ms**. | Causal evidence for phase-dependent action. The paper's approximately **1 ms nerve-to-muscle activation delay is an assumption used to interpret this result**, not a measurement of force-rise time. |
| [Beatus, Guckenheimer & Cohen 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4387536/), magnetic perturbation in free-flying Drosophila | Corrective wing-stroke asymmetry begins within **5 ms**; perturbations up to 100 degrees were corrected within **30 ± 7 ms**. | Constrains the complete reflex's speed. It does not identify a particular muscle's calcium or twitch kernel. |
| [Hürkey et al. 2023, Fig. 1 and Fig. 4](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06099-0), tethered Drosophila with DLM recordings, GCaMP8f imaging, and electrical/genetic manipulation | DLM MNs normally fire about **3–12 Hz**, once per approximately **20–40 wingbeats**. A spike-triggered DLM calcium signal rises with **tau = 6.2 ± 1.7 ms** and decays with **tau = 82 ± 6.7 ms**. Synchronous MN spikes produce peak wingbeat-frequency changes after **7.3 ± 1.3 ms**, decaying with **tau = 83 ± 27 ms**. | These are identified DLM calcium and wingbeat-frequency measurements, not DVM measurements or direct force twitch constants. |

Hürkey et al. additionally measured DLM fibre-voltage decay components near **1.7 ms** and **14 ms**. Calcium was imaged at 98 Hz using GCaMP8f; the reported fitted calcium constants should retain that methodological context. Their normal DLM firing is staggered across motor units. Genetically forcing synchronous firing produced approximately **eightfold larger wingbeat-frequency fluctuations**. Thus asynchronous mechanical contraction does not make spike timing across the power MN population irrelevant. Their electrically stimulated DLM calcium responses were tested at **0.5, 1, 2, 5, and 10 Hz**, with summation beginning at 2 Hz. [Primary study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06099-0)

## A slower power envelope is a different measurement

[Gordon & Dickinson 2006](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1449689/) recorded five DLM fibres and all three DVM groups, with 111 experiments in tethered Drosophila responding to moving visual patterns. Approximate step changes in spike rate had fitted rise/fall time constants **0.134/0.329 s**, while estimated mechanical power changed with **0.442/1.79 s** time constants. This supports a distinction between neural drive and the power envelope. These behavioral/ensemble fits are **not** direct single-spike calcium or force constants and should not replace the newer DLM measurements above. Fast steering-induced wing-amplitude changes also contribute to observed power/wing-frequency dynamics.

## Species and force-kinetics caveat

[Tu & Dickinson 1994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9317652/) measured b1 work loops in **Calliphora vicina**, not Drosophila. At 150 Hz, b1 performed net negative work and its mechanical effect depended on activation phase. Phase-sensitive steering therefore need not mean a muscle completely contracts and relaxes within one stroke; modulation of active stiffness and work can matter. This bounded search did **not** establish validated force-rise/decay kernels for all 12 Drosophila steering types.

## Implications for the current model

The current neural state uses a **50 ms rate EMA** (`packages/banc-runtime/src/neural.wgsl`, state field 4). Training reads that rate every **2 ms** from a neural simulation with **0.5 ms** timesteps. The body averages mapped rates before muscle excitation and uses common muscle kinetics. A 50 ms time constant spans roughly 10–12 wingbeats, so this representation suppresses the phase information demonstrated above. Reducing one downstream time constant cannot recover information already collapsed upstream.

An **80 Hz saturation threshold is not a common biological normalization**: b1 can fire once per approximately 200 Hz stroke, b2 bursts around 100 Hz, whereas the identified DLM MN working range in Hürkey et al. is approximately 3–12 Hz. A model DLM rate near 90 Hz therefore calls for neural calibration and raw spike-count verification, rather than an implicit rescaling to a desired force. Rate estimates and transient startup windows should not be equated with sustained experimental firing ranges without checking their definitions.

## Smallest explicit opt-in temporal interface proposal

1. Keep the existing rate-only path exactly unchanged. Add an explicit opt-in event mode carrying the covered neural interval, motor identities, event timestamps, and rates. Rates remain available for diagnostics and legacy consumers; temporal-mode muscle updates must not count both events and rates as excitation.
2. For the current bounded configuration, read each motor neuron's cumulative spike count and last-spike timestamp. This is sufficient only when the enforced minimum inter-spike interval exceeds the observed block duration, the previous count is known, and no read interval is skipped. The current implementation plan uses 2 ms blocks and a 2 ms refractory period with the existing 0.5 ms tick semantics. Reject unsafe configurations, missing intervals, impossible timestamps, and count deltas greater than one; do not silently interpolate. This uses the per-neuron state, not the global recent-spike ring. If larger blocks or shorter refractory periods are later supported, use a per-MN event bitmap or another explicitly lossless representation instead. At four ticks per block, 805 uint32 bitmasks would require about 3.2 KB per readback.
3. Deliver events to the body at their actual offsets within the matching physical interval. Preserve global neuron identity through the IO mapping, left/right identity, distinct DLM motor units, and event ordering. Retain the actual mechanical wing phase for synchronous steering events. The existing 0.5 ms tick is the timing resolution; do not invent sub-tick precision.
4. In a subsequent, separately calibrated effector implementation, use per-spike synchronous steering activation tied to muscle/hinge mechanics and per-power-unit calcium states whose aggregate drive can preserve staggered DLM firing. The evidence does not yet justify numeric steering force kernels or transferring DLM constants directly to DVM. Exposing timestamps alone does not validate such a force model.
5. Reject incomplete/overflowed event batches; never silently synthesize spikes from rates. Last-spike time alone is insufficient without the count and interval guards above. An unchecked global recent-spike ring is also insufficient for lossless motor transport.

Suggested interface checks before any training: same event count at different phases produces distinct timing at the muscle boundary; equal-rate staggered versus synchronous DLM trains remain distinguishable; all events in a block survive; batching does not move event times; zero events remain zero; legacy rate-mode replay remains unchanged. None of these plumbing checks alone would establish stable flight or biological calibration.

## Group-specific effector priors: what is justified now

| Group | Defensible next model boundary | What remains unmeasured or unvalidated |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DLM asynchronous power units | Preserve the individual MN event trains before aggregating power. A separate calcium-like state per unit can use the measured 6.2 ms rise and 82 ms decay as an explicit prior. Let the neural circuit determine the actual staggering. | The observed GCaMP signal is not a measured force impulse response. Converting that state to thoracic power, stretch activation, and wingbeat frequency needs its own calibration. A fitted fluorescence response does not uniquely determine a double-exponential kernel or its amplitude. |
| DVM asynchronous power units | Preserve events per unit and expose a separately documented family configuration. | The 2023 constants above were measured in DLM. Reusing them for DVM would be a homology assumption, not direct DVM physiology. |
| Synchronous steering muscles | Deliver each event at its simulation-resolved time, retain the mechanical phase at that time, and keep the muscle's existing identity, side, and force geometry. Test phase responses with controlled spike trains before selecting an effector model. | No validated Drosophila force kernel for all 12 mapped types was established here. Fast afferent-to-EMG latency and phase-dependent kinematics do not identify a force rise/decay pair. In particular, neither a universal 1 ms twitch nor the current 15/40 ms activation constants follows from these papers. b1 may require active stiffness/length/velocity dependence rather than only a positive force pulse. |

The smallest evidence-supported change is therefore **temporal transport with preserved identities**, followed by explicitly labeled family-specific effector experiments. Do not reset the oscillator at a motor spike, assign each steering type a desired corrective phase, coordinate DLM events outside the neural model, or upscale/downscale rates to make observed behavior look correct. Such steps would add controllers or calibration assumptions beyond preserving the simulated neural signal.
