# Why the fly falls off the fruit

The observed sequence is **sliding → falling → wing/surface impacts → tumbling**. The current run does not show a supported, controlled takeoff. Ground information reaches the sensory neurons, but the body and actuator adapter do not turn the current outputs into stable stance and flight.

## One-fly live trace

The [live audit](flybody-flight-live-audit.json) records 610 simulated milliseconds with the full BANC brain, rendered eyes, original 3D console and native MuJoCo body. A read-only hook records native body state every 1 ms. There are no injected motor commands, root forces or stabilizers. The report contains 610 detailed physical samples and the ordinary neural/muscle snapshots. No runtime error occurred.

| Simulated time | Observation |
|---|---|
| 11 ms | The body is sliding and descending while both wing activations are exactly zero. |
| 36 ms | Mean wing activation first exceeds 0.05. The body was already moving downhill. |
| 136 ms | First classified airborne: vertical velocity −160 mm/s, body tilt 64°, mean wing activation 0.68. This departure is downward. |
| 167 ms | First recorded wing/ground impact. Vertical constraint force is about 77 times body weight, while aerodynamic vertical force is about 0.17 times weight at that sample. The vertical velocity is now upward, +121 mm/s. |
| 460 ms | A later wing impact coincides with angular speed of 1,904 rad/s. |

The 1 ms trace samples contact state after each body control interval; it can miss contacts between those samples. These times are first recorded events, not substep-exact onset times. The existing near-ground airborne threshold also excludes brief unloaded states with the feet still close to the surface. Constraint forces include native contact/limit forces; the detailed samples identify the simultaneous wing collisions. The instantaneous air forces include drag as well as lift.

## Controls that isolate the cause

The [mechanical replay](flybody-flight-current-ablation.json) uses the same recorded motor-rate time series for each case, one body at a time, for 400 ms. It is a mechanics experiment, not a second autonomous neural trial.

| Intervention | Result |
|---|---|
| All motor input zero | Slides off and overturns; maximum angular speed 93 rad/s. The fall does not require a neural flight command. |
| Recorded input, wings disabled | Still falls; maximum angular speed 89 rad/s. |
| Recorded wing input only | Wing/ground impacts and tumbling; maximum angular speed 1,563 rad/s. |
| All recorded motor input | Wing/ground impacts and tumbling; maximum angular speed 1,295 rad/s. |

This isolates two problems: passive support on the curved fruit is insufficient, and active wings exacerbate the fall through collision impulses. It does not identify a unique stance/friction parameter that fixes the first problem. Earlier increases in friction and wing torque limits did not reliably solve active behavior and remain disabled.

## The wings cannot support the current launch posture

The [restrained-body force audit](flybody-flight-force-audit.json) holds the root and non-wing joints fixed above the ground, runs the same native wing servo and measured 218 Hz waveform, and measures aerodynamic force. This artificial restraint is confined to the diagnostic; it is never applied to the production fly. Each run lasts 200 ms and discards the first 50 ms. The values below are mean vertical force divided by body weight, with steering offsets zero.

| Wing activation | Level body | Body pitched 47.5° nose-up |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0.25 | −0.018 | −0.021 |
| 0.50 | −0.006 | −0.040 |
| 0.75 | 0.348 | 0.399 |
| 1.00 | 0.590 | 1.047 |

At full activation in the level posture, the forward aerodynamic force is 0.879 times body weight. Thus visibly vigorous flapping can push the body forward while failing to oppose gravity. At partial activation, the adapter scales **all three wing angles from the folded resting pose**, changing stroke amplitude, mean orientation and wing rotation together. “75% activation” is not “75% of hover lift.” The high-activation, pitched-body result only establishes sufficient mean lift in a restrained test; it does not establish free-flight stability.

## Steering information is being discarded

`web/flybody-physics.js` combines the different b1/b2/b3, i1/i2, iii and iv steering-muscle outputs into one mean per side and adds a positive 0.05-radian yaw offset. There is no calibrated mapping from those separate motor units to stroke timing, wing rotation and pitch/roll/yaw moments. A correction generated by the neural graph cannot retain its muscle-specific mechanical effect through that averaging.

The upstream [FlyBody flight task](https://github.com/TuragaLab/flybody/blob/d015e9bfe441bd90ae431bac24c55cb74bdbce26/flybody/tasks/flight_imitation.py) combines its wing waveform with learned residual actions. Its [flight base configuration](https://github.com/TuragaLab/flybody/blob/d015e9bfe441bd90ae431bac24c55cb74bdbce26/flybody/tasks/base.py) normally disables floor collisions and retracts the legs. We imported the native mechanics and waveform, not a trained takeoff/stabilization policy. The waveform alone is therefore not evidence of a working flight controller.

## Change made in this audit

The original UI and movement modes remain. Direct native-body motion now reports **“Airborne · wings active”**, **“Moving on surface”** or **“Overturned”** when those are the measured facts. It no longer labels any wing-active fall “Flying” or any passive slide “Walking.” The one-fly console validation passed after this change. The separate stage monitor still reports explicitly defined proxies; its labels are not proof of a successful flight sequence.

The repair order supported by these measurements is: establish passive support on the curved fruit with motors disconnected; calibrate motor activation against actual wing force while separating deployment from the beat waveform; preserve individual steering-muscle effects and fit their moments; then test takeoff and corrective responses with the full neural loop. No unvalidated change to flight forces, friction or neural physiology was enabled during this audit.
