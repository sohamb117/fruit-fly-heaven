      How the brain drives this body

      The default Behavior signals controller restores the original walking rule: DNp09 activity plus 15% of combined DNa01/DNa02 steering activity supplies forward drive. Left–right steering sets yaw; proboscis activity slows walking. This is a behavior-level interpretation of actual WASM firing rates. Direct motor neurons instead uses separate forward and steering channels.

      Both controllers retain the 12 channels below, using the same 97 annotated neurons, for the remaining body outputs and inspection. Direct actuator gains have a 2 Hz dead zone and reach full drive at 42 Hz. Behavior walking uses smooth, saturating gains without that dead zone. All gains, force scales, and joint patterns are modeling assumptions.

      Behavior mode includes an explicit flight program: locomotor activity accumulates preparation, body feedback stabilizes lift, and a bounded flight bout ends in landing. Direct mode omits this assistance. There are no random flight commands or chosen destinations. A falling body can keep moving through inertia and gravity after motor input stops. Rendered eyes and a body-sense boundary adapter provide sensory feedback.

      What is real, and what is modeled?

      The wiring is real.

      Every one of the source model’s 15,091,983 directed connection rows is included, representing 54,492,922 synapses. The flies share this immutable wiring; their voltages, synaptic currents, spike histories, and random inputs are independent.

      The downloaded v783 computational model contains 138,639 neurons, compared with 139,255 in FlyWire’s classification. No additional neurons or connections were removed here.

      The electrical activity is a model.

      Reference mode uses Float64 state, a 0.1 ms threshold grid, 20 ms membrane decay, 5 ms synaptic decay, 1.8 ms transmission delay, and a 2.2 ms refractory period. Fast mode uses Float32 state, a 1 ms grid, and 2 ms delay/refractory periods. This approximation can change spike timing and activity. Neurotransmitter signs are predictions supplied by the source model.

      The event-based engine analytically updates quiet cells when needed. Every neural step is accounted for; rendering does not advance the neural clock.

      The habitat is supplied.

      Fruit odor stimulates measured DM1/VA2 olfactory cells; contact stimulates sugar/water sensory cells. Concentration and firing-rate mappings are assumptions. The default behavior controller combines walking, steering, and feeding activity to set ground movement. The optional direct controller reads separate forward and steering outputs. Identified brain outputs supply reverse drive, wing power, takeoff, landing extension, grooming, proboscis, and antenna actuation in both modes. The body decoder supplies rate mappings, force gains, joint patterns, and a behavior-mode flight program. Flight preparation is driven by locomotor activity; sustained wings and landing are modeled body control. No ventral nerve cord is loaded.

      The default body clock follows neural time. The optional Live setting holds the latest output on a wall-clock body simulation. Position and fruit contact feed back into sensory inputs. Two head-mounted grayscale cameras stimulate mapped R1–6 photoreceptors and a separate trained graded visual network. Graded T2/T3 and T4/T5 outputs enter matching visual neurons in the full FlyWire graph. Joint posture, motion, contact and antennal deflection feed a documented body-sense approximation at existing sensory and ascending neurons. Vision and Body sense can be disconnected independently; neither writes motor commands. Motor coupling disconnects actuator inputs while the brain keeps computing; Pause habitat freezes both clocks.

      Food is inexhaustible. Metabolism, reproduction, learning, and subjective experience are not modeled. This is not a validated digital replica of a living fly.
